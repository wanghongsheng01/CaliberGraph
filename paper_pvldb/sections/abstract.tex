\begin{abstract}
Execution accuracy (EX), the standard metric of Text-to-SQL benchmarks, accepts a predicted query if it returns the gold result on one database, and recent verifiers report that many accepted predictions are not equivalent to the gold query. We study what such counterexample databases prove, with three pre-registered stages on \V{nExc} accepted BIRD predictions of ten systems, thirteen further systems and Spider. Under one SQLite certifier, budget and set of admissibility rules, we compare five disprover families, including CaliberGraph, which builds candidates along the join graph. Counts depend on the rules: almost no witness admissible under DDL- and format-derived rules satisfies a data profile of the benchmark instance (domains, nullability, unique column combinations, inclusion and value dependencies). Whether a witness is evidence also depends on who judges it with what data. Two human annotators shown facts about the benchmark data judged most schema-tier witnesses on which they agreed (\V{hvTwoImpConsOne} of \V{hvTwoConsOne}) to rest on implausible data; without those facts they almost never did; item by item they agreed only moderately with the LLM annotator that labelled our full samples ($\kappa=\V{hvTwoKappaAIbinMax}$), and a repair with real values removed the difference for \V{xVanPct}\,\% of the witnesses. At the profile tier the implausible share falls, and CaliberGraph-R, which searches real sub-databases first, certifies more predictions than random fuzzing with the same data access on BIRD and on thirteen new systems; on Spider, whose small instances yield a weaker profile, it does not, and fuzzing's witnesses are more often meaningful. The effect of witnesses on EX lies between \V{ptsErrRMin}--\V{ptsErrOnefMax} points per system, weighting them by attributed error rates (a lower bound), and 15--20 points, counting every schema-tier witness as an error. We report every pre-registered outcome, including the failed ones, and release all artifacts.
\end{abstract}
